Research Focus

Sparse sensing and spatial reconstruction

Building measurements are inherently sparse and physically coupled — whether separating 3D translation from torsion in structural motion, or resolving coupled airflow and heat transfer across a room from a few point sensors. By embedding physical priors, this work decouples these effects to reconstruct full 3D fields from minimal observations.

Structural monitoring

Three-dimensional floor motion during earthquakes

Wiring every floor is rarely practical, and a single camera view can make translation and torsion difficult to distinguish. Combining synchronized views with structural mechanics allows engineers to recover how each floor moves and twists during shaking.

Building physics

Three-dimensional reconstruction of indoor thermal environments

A few wall thermostats reveal little about temperature and airflow where people sit and work. Interpolating their readings alone cannot capture the influence of room geometry and heat transfer. Combining 3D room reconstruction with sparse measurements makes it possible to map conditions throughout the occupied space and inform operating decisions that balance comfort and energy use.

Verifiable AI for design and construction

As AI capabilities rapidly advance, applying them to civil engineering depends on whether every decision can be verified against explicit rules and evidence. This research grounds AI in enforceable standards: governing building codes and mechanics calculations for structural design, and site safety codes and operational rules for robotic construction.

Construction robotics

Adaptive lift planning on active construction sites

On an active jobsite, a newly placed component can obstruct a planned lift, and a worker can enter the crane’s operating zone. Linking visual site observations to lift planning allows the crane to adjust its path as work progresses and pause when personnel enter the lifting area.

Engineering design and AI

Traceable code checks for structural design

Structural design requires both the correct interpretation of a code clause and a reliable calculation. A language model locates applicable provisions and organizes the checks, while deterministic solvers perform the mechanics calculations. Engineers can trace a proposed design back to its assumptions, clauses, and numerical results before signing off.
